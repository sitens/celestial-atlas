About · sources · self-test

Every pixel and number is a pure function of one master timestamp. Positions come from Astronomy Engine (Don Cross, MIT) — VSOP87-class planets, a Moon theory, Jupiter's Galilean moons — accurate to about an arcminute for 1700–2200 CE. Other moons use JPL SSD mean elements (epoch J2000; “mean-element accuracy”: good for visuals, not for precise phenomena). Planet orientation uses IAU WGCCRE rotation models. Eclipse shadows are computed analytically per pixel from the true Sun/occluder geometry (exact disc-overlap), so umbra and penumbra land where the ephemeris says. Eclipse times/contacts come from Astronomy Engine's search routines, not from animation.

Limits. 1700–2200 CE: no warning. 1000–1700 and 2200–3000: allowed with a “reduced accuracy” banner. Outside 1000–3000 the picker is disabled. Past dates show the proleptic Gregorian calendar; local time uses today's IANA rules so pre-1970 civil times may differ from history. Star positions are J2000 precessed to the date (no proper motion). Earth city-lights and cloud textures are present-day. Planet surfaces, ring shadows and moon shadows on Jupiter use real geometry, but body sizes in “Explorable” scale are exaggerated.

Data & credits. Geocoding & weather: Open-Meteo (CC-BY 4.0; forecast, ERA5 historical archive). Planet & Milky Way textures: Solar System Scope (CC BY 4.0, NASA-derived). Earth, Moon, clouds, city lights: three.js example textures (NASA Visible Earth / Blue Marble derived). Hubble galaxies: NASA/ESA (via Wikimedia Commons): NGC 4414, M51, NGC 1300, M104, Antennae. Star & constellation data: Yale Bright Star Catalogue via d3-celestial (BSD). Satellite elements: NASA/JPL SSD. Rendering: three.js (MIT).

Helical motion (Solar System view, “Helix” chip). The Sun carries the whole system at ≈ 246 km/s (≈ 52 AU every year) around the Galactic Centre, toward Galactic l ≈ 87°, b ≈ 2° (Cygnus). In the Galaxy's frame each planet therefore traces a corkscrew (the Moon a smaller one around Earth's). Trails are real positions from the ephemeris, displaced along the Sun's velocity; the along-track stretch is exaggerated (slider) because the true pitch is ≈ 52× the orbit radius — the panel states the factor. In “True scale” the helix is drawn at its real length. In the default scale the Moon's orbit is drawn compact from afar (so it can't cross the orbits of Venus or Mars) and opens to full size as you approach Earth.

Beyond the Milky Way. The NASA/JPL-Caltech/R. Hurt (SSC-Caltech) artist's concept of the Milky Way (public domain, via Wikimedia Commons) is shown beneath the model stars (toggle “NASA art”); it is an illustration, not a photograph — nobody has seen our Galaxy from outside. Spiral arms rotate rigidly at an adjustable pattern speed Ωp (20–32 km/s/kpc; the literature spans this range), and the strip shows when the Sun would be inside an arm (half-width 0.5 kpc, model-dependent). Any link between arm passages and mass extinctions or ice ages is a hypothesis, not a settled result. Neighbour and Local Group positions are catalogue values (McConnachie 2012; NED); the Laniakea view is schematic: cluster positions are catalogue values, the filaments and streamlines are illustrative of the flow toward the Great Attractor (Tully et al. 2014).

Galaxy view. A schematic Milky Way: 110,000 procedural stars on four logarithmic spiral arms (13° pitch, crossing the Sun line near Reid+2019 positions), a 27° bar and a bulge. The Sun's orbit is integrated in a flat-rotation-curve potential (R₀ 8.18 kpc, vc 233 km/s, solar motion U,V,W = 11.1, 12.24, 7.25 km/s): one galactic year ≈ 223 Myr, radial swing 8.1–9.2 kpc, vertical oscillation ±85 pc every ≈ 70 Myr. The vertical wobble is multiplied (slider) so it is visible; the disc is drawn thicker than reality. “Helix through space” adds the Galaxy's ≈ 560 km/s motion relative to the CMB (Sun’s 370 km/s dipole minus its orbital velocity; toward l ≈ 266°, b ≈ 29°) — the path then winds with a pitch of ≈ 125 kpc per galactic year (drawn at 20% by default so the coils show; the Drift slider goes to the real 100%).
